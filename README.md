# 🔍 Analysis
## 1. New vs Returning Users

### Objective
To compare revenue contribution and purchasing behavior between new and returning users.

### Key Findings

* New users (80,106) generated higher total revenue (6.9M) due to a significantly larger user base. 
* Returning users (29,970) placed 45,165 orders — averaging 1.51 orders per user, compared to 1.00 for new users. 
* Average price per item remained nearly identical: 59.45 (New) vs 59.75 (Returning).

### Insight
Revenue differences between new and returning users are not driven by spending habits per transaction, but by purchase frequency. Returning users spend the same amount per item — they simply come back more often.

### Additional Observation
When analyzing a smaller time range, results appeared heavily skewed toward new users. 
Expanding the range revealed a more balanced pattern, suggesting that early-stage data can overrepresent new user activity. Long-term data provides a more accurate view of user behavior.

## 2. Category Analysis

### Objective
To identify which product categories drive revenue and how purchasing behavior differs across categories.

### Key Findings

Outerwear & Coats generated the highest revenue (1.3M) with only 9,017 purchases.
Intimates recorded the highest purchase volume (13,586) but produced only 454K in revenue.
Jeans ranked second in revenue (1.2M) while maintaining strong purchase volume (12,774), acting as a core category.
Suits showed a high average price (117) but low purchase volume (1,121), indicating potential for growth.

### Insight
Revenue is not driven by purchase volume alone.
The contrast between Outerwear and Intimates clearly shows that price plays a more significant role than quantity.

The product structure can be segmented into three groups:

* High-value categories (e.g., Outerwear & Coats)
* Balanced categories (e.g., Jeans)
* Volume-driven categories (e.g., Intimates, Swim)


## 3. Price Analysis

### Objective
To analyze how different price segments influence purchasing behavior and revenue.

### Key Findings

* High-priced items generated 5.6M in revenue from 36,790 purchases (avg 152.89).
* Mid-priced items generated 3.9M from 77,772 purchases (avg 50.32).
* Low-priced items generated 1.3M from 67,258 purchases (avg 19.19).

### Insight
The pricing structure clearly separates:

* Volume drivers (Low price)
* Core revenue stabilizers (Mid price)
* Revenue drivers (High price)

Mid-priced items have 2x more purchases than high-priced items, yet high-priced items generate 1.4x more revenue — fewer transactions, significantly higher value.

## Key Takeaway

Across all analyses, one consistent principle emerges:

* Revenue is driven by price and purchase frequency — not volume alone.

This is supported by:

* Returning users purchase 1.51x more frequently than new users
* Outerwear (9K purchases) generates 3x more revenue than Intimates (13K purchases)
* High-priced items generate significantly higher revenue despite fewer transactions


## 💼 Business Implications

The analysis suggests that optimizing for transaction volume alone is insufficient.
Instead, effective revenue growth requires a combined focus on:

* User Retention (Frequency)
* Returning users demonstrate stronger engagement through repeated purchases
* Increasing retention directly improves revenue efficiency
* High-Value Pricing Strategy
* Revenue is disproportionately driven by high-priced items
* Encouraging purchases in higher price segments is critical


## 🚀 Actionable Strategy

The most effective strategy lies at the intersection of the two strongest revenue drivers:

👉 Returning users + High-price segment

To leverage this:

* Offer loyalty-based rewards to increase repeat purchases
* Provide early access to premium products to reinforce high-value behavior
* Apply personalized promotions based on past purchasing categories

This approach maximizes:

* Purchase frequency
* Customer lifetime value (LTV)
* Long-term revenue sustainability

## 🧠 Conclusion

This analysis demonstrates that:

* Revenue growth is not a function of volume alone, but of value and repetition
* High-priced products and returning users are the primary revenue drivers
* A combined strategy of retention and premium targeting is essential for sustainable business performance
